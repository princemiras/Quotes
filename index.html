    6 Comfort 🏡

    Words from home

    How do I tell my parents I'm struggling?

    1. Pick a calm moment. After dinner or when things are quiet is usually easier.
    2. Start with what you really feel. “Mom, Dad, I'm so tired and I'm scared about my grades.” That's enough.
    3. Ask for what you need. For example: “Can you just listen first?” or “Can you help me find a tutor?”

    If home doesn't feel comfortable to talk, it's okay to find another trusted person: a teacher, a guidance counselor, an aunt or uncle, or a close friend.

    When you're compared or put down at home

    1. Pause before you react. Breathe in for 4, out for 6. You don't have to win any argument tonight.
    2. Don't take the comparison as the truth. It measures someone else's path. It says nothing about your value.
    3. Say it when things are calm. Try: “Mom, Dad, I want to do well, but being compared makes me feel worse, not better. Can you help me instead?”
    4. Find your safe person. A teacher, counselor, relative or friend who can listen to what home is like for you.

    Many parents compare because they're afraid for your future or carry their own pressure. That can explain it, but it doesn't make it okay, and it doesn't make you less. If you ever feel unsafe, or feel like you can't go on, tell a trusted adult right away or call the NCMH Crisis Hotline, open 24/7: 1553 or 1800-1888-1553 (Smart/TNT 0919-057-1553, Globe/TM 0917-899-8727).

        8 Together 🤝

        You don't have to carry it alone

        Quotes can lift you up, but real people can sit beside you. Here's who to turn to and how to start.

        People close to you

        It doesn't have to be a big conversation to start.

        • 👩‍🏫 A guidance counselor or school adviser
        • 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 A close friend or classmate you trust
        • 👨‍👩‍👧 A parent, aunt or uncle, or an older sibling

        Ways to start the conversation

        Borrow these words if you're not sure what to say.

        • “Do you have a few minutes? I've been having a hard time.”
        • “I don't need advice, I just need someone to listen.”
        • “Can we study together? I'm feeling overwhelmed.”

        You don't have to be perfect. Showing up is enough. 💛
